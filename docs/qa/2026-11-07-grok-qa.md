# First-time-user QA, 07 Nov 2026

> Source: Grok, a first-time-user pass over the production console and the shared Arc Studio demos, 07 Nov 2026, Asia/Calcutta. The full report is [2026-11-07-grok-qa-report.pdf](2026-11-07-grok-qa-report.pdf). This page summarises it, and [HANDOFF-rajdeep.md](HANDOFF-rajdeep.md) turns it into copy changes.

Testnet only. The tester moved no funds, signed nothing, created no account and installed no wallet extension.

## Verdict

**No: the console is not self-explanatory enough for a stranger's first impression today.**

- A stranger can open the console, read the pitch, and browse Registry, Assistant, Seller, Merchant, Start and Verify without an account.
- The first impression fails on two points:
  - **Onboarding clarity.** The Buyer screen asks for an approval token and never says how to get one.
  - **Rail scope.** The landing says "3 rails live" while the Registry shows five rail filters.
- Browsing and verifying largely work. Verify read a slip as `VALID` with its release on chain, and read the same slip with its amount doubled as `INVALID`.
- Not tested: a full purchase, because sign-in stopped at Privy's email step, and a verify with the network disconnected.

## Withdrawn or not confirmed

The tester raised these, then withdrew them or could not confirm them:

- **A false automatic-refund claim on a direct rail.** Not confirmed. The escrow terms say anyone can refund after the deadline.
- **The Hold control is broken.** False. It succeeds on a true press of two seconds or more.
- **The Buyer tabs are broken.** Not confirmed. The screen is behind the token gate.
- **The changing demo offer hash proves a fake published offer.** Not established. It fits an unlabelled sample regenerated on each load.

The report also lists claims it could not check from the browser, without calling them false: the live counters and seller figures come from the hub, and "43 Foundry tests" was not checked.

## Missing in the first two minutes (section E)

1. A plain explanation of the envelope and the approval token: what they are, who creates them, and how a first-time person gets a token onto the Buyer screen.
2. Who can trigger a refund, on which rail, and when: the actor at the deadline, not only "2 min".
3. One consistent map of live, listed and declared rails.
4. Seller display names, or a stated policy that sellers are anonymous.
5. A demo label on the Arc Studio share and the Vercel app, so the ready 4.50 sample is not read as a live listing.
6. A short "what happens to the money" strip that ties together the amount, the rail, the deadline, who can refund, and the slip.
